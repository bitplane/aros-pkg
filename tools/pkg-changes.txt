Every verb says what it takes, as an AmigaDOS command does: pkg ? lists the templates, pkg <verb> ? shows one, and pkg HELP MACHINE is pkg's whole account of its grammar for a program. A word a verb does not take is refused by name, where it used to be accepted and ignored: PUSH ... DRYRUN pushed for real, and twelve other verbs took DRYRUN without honouring it.
A slip is caught before anything is chosen: a keyword misspelt (ROTO: did you mean ROOT?), a path or a word in capitals where a package name belongs, a keyword where its value was forgotten, an empty value. None of them reaches the default environment's root any more.
Channels take short names (CHANNEL pkg), pkg VERSION answers, pkg U looks before it asks for administrator access, and a result is drawn in the project's purple, a question on a line of its own.
Inside, the library is eleven files instead of one of eleven thousand lines, and every build, Windows and both AROS CPUs included, is checked at each change.
